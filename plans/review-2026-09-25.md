# Tessera quality and scale review

Read-only review begun while `detector-wide-v17` trained. V17 completed on 2026-09-26;
the real-document results below were recorded after training. No source changes or tests
were made for this review.

## V17 outcome (2026-09-26)

The scripted four-epoch run completed training, quantization, export, real-document
detector evaluation, and real-address parser evaluation. The quantization gate passed.

## What the current evidence says

The strongest measured gap is between synthetic validation and real documents. V16
reached 97.1% macro exact F1 on its synthetic validation set
(`runs/detector-wide-v16/summary.json`), while its real-document exact F1 includes UK
people at 63.2%, Georgian people at 56.3%, and Georgian addresses at 30.9%
(`internal/plans/HANDOFF.md`, section 3). The wider model made small gains, but it did not
close the country-level gaps. V17's mixed real-document results confirm that higher
synthetic scores do not by themselves establish the release targets.

At 100 countries, the current one-model design does not run 20 times per document.
Training and verification are the immediate scale limits. The synthetic generator has a
fixed 170,000-document train budget: approximately 34,000 per country across five, or
1,700 per country across 100 if unchanged. Maintaining today's allocation requires
about 3.4 million synthetic documents, plus real labels and evaluation for each country.
The generator currently accepts six country codes; country-specific inputs are scattered.
The shared 32,768 × 48 n-gram table and 13 script rows are potential representation
limits, but their effect on accuracy is unmeasured.

Widening the detector from 96 to 192 channels raises approximate per-token dense work
from 174,912 to 681,600 multiply-accumulates, about 3.9 times. The shipped 96-wide
browser profile took about 94.5 ms on a 10,000-character Chrome fixture. Applying the
arithmetic ratio to its detector stage suggests roughly 337 ms for a wide model, but this
is an estimate, not a browser measurement of v16 or v17. The project target is 150 ms.

The shipped detector evaluation defines an address as “found” on any same-kind byte
overlap (`trainer/src/detect_eval.rs:339-374`), while synthetic detector lenient scoring
requires intersection-over-union of at least 0.5 (`trainer/src/detector.rs:424-455`).
These are different metrics. The release plan also tracks exact span F1, so this is a
reporting caveat rather than proof that a target has been met incorrectly.

## Storage cleanup assessment

Before cleanup, `target/` occupied about 71 GB: `target/debug/incremental` was 19 GB,
`target/debug/deps` 35 GB, `target/wasm32-unknown-unknown` 5.4 GB, and
`target/release` 8.5 GB. The v17 trainer ran from `target/release`, and its
script called that release binary for quantization and evaluation. The 19 GB
`target/debug/incremental` cache was removed. It is rebuildable and does not affect
the running release binary, but later debug compilation may take longer. Removing all
of `target/debug` would have recovered about 55 GB before this cleanup and require a full
debug rebuild. `data/raw/libpostal` is 7.5 GB and is needed to prepare new parser
samples; `runs/old` is 884 MB of historical model artifacts. Neither is a first
choice during training. After cache removal, the filesystem reported at least 127 GiB
available; the trainer remained active and later completed. Filesystem availability may also change due to
macOS purgeable space, so that difference should not be attributed solely to this cache.

## Hypotheses to test

- The frozen parser n-gram table may limit detector adaptation. The detector imports it
  through `ngram_from` and keeps it frozen (`trainer/src/train.rs:255-258,305-325`), while
  all scripts hash into 32,768 rows (`tessera/src/features.rs:115-139`). Compare frozen
  shared, separately trainable, and larger-table variants on the same real dev sources.
- The tenfold silver repeat may overemphasize familiar publishers. Compare repeat rates,
  source-balanced sampling, and targeted address data with model size held constant.
- A single global model may or may not remain the best serving design at 100 countries.
  Compare it with language-group or optional country packs only after measuring transfer
  on new publishers and contrasting scripts.
- Parser component decoding is greedy (`tessera/src/model/bio.rs:204-223`), while the detector
  uses Viterbi over legal sequences (`bio.rs:35-49,61-118`). Sequence decoding could recover
  components lost after a local `O` decision, but a real-address comparison is needed before
  changing outputs or confidence semantics.

## Recommended order

1. Use v17's completed real-document results, then measure its latency on the same
   browser profile fixture before judging its accuracy/latency trade-off.
2. Treat the repeatedly inspected gold files as development data. Reserve new publishers
   as a sealed final test, and set checkpoint/experiment objectives to the actual person,
   address, and parser targets per country.
3. Gate silver export on unresolved labels. Measure errors by source and label; collect
   more *distinct target examples* where gaps are largest, especially Georgian addresses.
4. Run controlled sampling and representation ablations with a fixed dev/test protocol.
5. Implement the already planned country registry, per-country budgets, and a local
   quality gate before attempting broad expansion.
6. Add real contact membership labels and score exact-contact precision and recall before
   using contact accuracy as a product claim. Audit script coverage before selecting the
   next countries.

## Review limits

No code was changed or tests/benchmarks run during this audit while v17 was training.
The wide-model latency and the effects of rebalance or larger embeddings are estimates
or hypotheses. The reviewer did not relabel a sample of silver data or inspect every
phone-region branch or verify the incidence of malformed Parquet shards and valid/test
component overlap. The second pass rejected a proposed parser-veto restoration because
the veto was deliberately removed. It also found no evidence that country count
multiplies bundle loading or model inference; there is one shared model. The third pass
did not run the site, test default-mode phones on the gold set, or measure parser Viterbi.
